====================================================================================================
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROBLEM STATEMENT
====================================================================================================
Conventional CCTV systems across public infrastructure, educational campuses, transit hubs, and
commercial facilities are almost entirely passive: footage is encoded to digital video recorders
(DVRs/NVRs) and reviewed only post-factum, long after an armed robbery, assault, or active shooter
event has concluded.

Real-time human monitoring of multi-camera walls suffers from severe cognitive vulnerability:
empirical studies in visual vigilance prove that human detection accuracy drops by over 73%
after only 20 minutes of continuous surveillance screen monitoring. Subtle threats, such as a
concealed blade or a silent physical altercation in a corridor, routinely escape human notice.

FAST-TRACK AI solves this critical vulnerability by providing an autonomous, real-time,
human-in-the-loop decision-support platform. It continuously processes live video streams (webcams,
RTSP feeds, and recorded video files), decouples spatial object detection from spatiotemporal
action classification, performs multi-frame tracking confirmation, triggers localized browser-based
acoustic alarms, generates cryptographically signed tamper-evident forensic evidence, and presents
actionable candidate incidents to human security operators inside an interactive Command Center.


====================================================================================================
2. CORE PHILOSOPHY & ETHICAL NON-GOALS
====================================================================================================
FAST-TRACK AI is governed by strict ethical and technical boundaries:

1. Prediction, Never Proof:
   All neural network outputs are strictly probabilistic indicators (e.g., "detected object: knife (0.78)"
   or "possible violence (0.65)"). The system never asserts guilt or definitive criminality.

2. Zero Biometrics / Zero Demographic Profiling:
   Facial recognition, identity inference, gender classification, racial profiling, and age estimation
   are strictly prohibited and completely absent from the codebase.

3. Zero Automated Police Dispatch:
   The system never directly summons emergency armed response automatically. All alerts remain internal
   candidate incidents requiring verified human operator confirmation before off-site escalation.

4. Privacy by Design:
   Automated facial anonymization (Haar Cascade + Gaussian Blurring) obfuscates human faces before keyframe
   evidence snapshots are committed to disk or rendered in forensic audit views.

5. Zero Secrets in Logs or Databases:
   RTSP credentials, connection tokens, and passwords in stream URLs are automatically sanitized upon
   ingestion (e.g., "rtsp://admin:secret@192.168.1.50" -> "rtsp://***@192.168.1.50").

6. Never Invent Metrics:
   Every chart, ROC curve, precision-recall curve, and confusion matrix rendered in the Command Center
   traces directly to static, reproducible evaluation artifacts stored in reports/eval/.

3.1 VIDEO INGESTION & EARLY PREPROCESSING
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Sources: Multi-format adapter supporting RTSP H.264/H.265 streams, local USB/integrated webcams
  (DirectShow on Windows / V4L2 on Linux), and pre-recorded MP4/AVI/MOV video files.
- Thread Isolation: Each camera feed runs on an isolated background capture thread (`CaptureWorker`).
- Reconnection Watchdog: Exponential backoff (1s, 2s, 4s, 8s, up to max 60s) automatically recovers
  dropped network streams without crashing the host application.
- Early Downscaling: Video inputs exceeding 1080p are downscaled immediately at ingestion to prevent
  excessive memory bus pressure.
- Drop-Oldest Queue: Frames enter a bounded queue (capacity = 2). If ML inference lags behind capture,
  stale frames are dropped immediately to guarantee zero operational latency.

3.2 IN-MEMORY JPEG RING BUFFER (PRE-ROLL / POST-ROLL)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Bounded Circular Buffer: Maintains compressed JPEG frames in RAM spanning the preceding 4.0 seconds.
- Memory Efficiency: Compressing frames to JPEG at 85% quality reduces RAM consumption by over 90%
  compared to raw BGR buffers.
- Pre-Roll Extraction: When an incident triggers, frames from before the alert are seamlessly stitched
  together with post-incident frames to produce a complete forensic video clip.

3.3 SPATIAL WEAPON DETECTION & BENIGN SUPPRESSION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Checkpoints:
  1. Fine-Tuned Threat Model (`weights/weapon_yolo.pt`): Specialized YOLO model detecting Gun (class 0),
     Explosion (class 1), Grenade (class 2), and Knife (class 3).
  2. Foundation Vision Model (`weights/yolo11n.pt`): 80-class COCO YOLO model for realistic full-scene
     understanding (Person, Cell Phone, Bottle, Knife, Scissors, etc.).
- Benign Object Suppression Engine:
  A major problem with weapon detection models is false alarms on everyday rectangular or slender objects
  (e.g., cell phones, pens, water bottles, remote controls). When running multi-class vision, if a candidate
  weapon bounding box overlaps significantly (IoU >= 0.35) with a recognized benign object (such as a
  cell phone or remote) and the benign object has higher or comparable confidence, the weapon proposal is
  automatically suppressed.
- Geometric Filtering:
  1. Minimum Box Area: Candidates below 400.0 square pixels (approx 20x20 px) are rejected as sub-pixel
     noise artifacts.
  2. Maximum Aspect Ratio: Extremities beyond 8.0:1 are discarded to eliminate linear shadow artifacts.

3.4 MULTI-OBJECT TRACKING & K-OF-N CANDIDATE CONFIRMATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ByteTrack Association: Uses Kalman filtering and high/low confidence association to maintain object
  identities across frames even under partial occlusion.
- K-of-N Temporal Confirmation: An object must be successfully detected in at least K out of the last N
  sampled frames (default: 2 out of 4 frames) before being promoted from an unconfirmed transient candidate
  to a confirmed threat alert. This eliminates single-frame optical flickers.

3.5 SPATIOTEMPORAL VIOLENCE CLASSIFICATION & KINETIC DYNAMICS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Architecture: 3D Convolutional Neural Network (TorchVision S3D / R3D-18) processing a rolling temporal
  window of 16 frames sampled at 8 Hz (covering a 2.0-second action window).
- Kinetic Motion Dynamics Differencing:
  To enhance physical altercation recognition, the system calculates normalized pixel intensity
  differencing between consecutive frames. Rapid struggles, flailing limbs, and physical shoving create
  elevated kinetic energy spikes, which dynamically modulate the final violence score.
- Temperature Scaling: Raw model logits are scaled by an empirical calibration factor (T = 1.28) to ensure
  calibrated probability outputs matching real-world precision.

3.6 TEMPORAL EVENT ENGINE & SEVERITY RULE TRIAGE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Polygon Spatial Zones: Operators draw Include zones (active detection areas) and Ignore zones (public
  sidewalks, foliage, roads) to eliminate out-of-perimeter false positives.
- Hysteresis Thresholding: Violence actions activate when score >= 0.65 (Ton) and only deactivate when
  score drops below 0.40 (Toff) across 2 consecutive windows, preventing rapid alert bouncing.
- Token-Bucket Rate Limiting: Limits event firing to a maximum of 6 alerts per minute per camera feed,
  protecting operators from alert fatigue during extended incidents.
- Discrete Severity Triage (Never Linear Score Summing):
  - HIGH: Confirmed Weapon AND Confirmed Violent Altercation occurring simultaneously on the same feed.
  - MEDIUM: Confirmed Weapon alone OR Confirmed Violent Altercation alone.
  - LOW: Unconfirmed transient candidate or low-confidence anomaly.
  - INFO: Operational system heartbeat, camera online/offline notice.

3.7 REAL-TIME AUDIO-VISUAL SECURITY ALARM SYSTEM
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Synthesized Browser Siren Buzzer:
  An embedded acoustic emergency warning tone synthesized via PCM WAV audio bytes and the HTML5 Web
  Audio API (AudioContext oscillator). Generates an unmistakable 880 Hz -> 440 Hz dual-frequency sweep.
  Requires zero external sound files and works universally across all web browsers without plugins.
- Operator Controls: Includes an audible siren toggle and an instant "Test Alarm Siren Sound" button.
- Flashing Tactical Alarm Banner: Renders a high-contrast pulsating banner above the video stream
  indicating the threat level (CRITICAL / HIGH), target labels (GUN / KNIFE), and dispatch status.
- Perimeter Alert Border: The surveillance video frame itself is bordered with a bold 4px crimson alert
  boundary whenever an active threat is detected.

3.8 CRYPTOGRAPHIC EVIDENCE SUBSYSTEM & CHAIN OF CUSTODY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Atomic Keyframe Snapshots: Whenever an alert triggers, the annotated video frame containing bounding
  boxes, confidence values, and timestamp is written to disk at `evidence/snapshots/{incident_id}_snapshot.jpg`.
- SHA-256 Digest: Upon file creation, an immutable cryptographic SHA-256 hash is computed and stored in
  the database. The Incident Detail page validates the physical file against the database digest in real
  time to guarantee that evidence has not been tampered with or corrupted (`Verified Match`).
- Facial Privacy Blurring: Applies automated Gaussian blurring across detected human faces before snapshots
  are rendered or exported.

3.9 MULTI-CHANNEL NOTIFICATION & QUIET-HOURS DISPATCH
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Webhook Integration: Sends formatted incident JSON payloads to external systems (Slack, Discord, MS Teams,
  corporate security dispatch).
- HMAC-SHA256 Signing: Every webhook request contains an `X-FastTrack-Signature` header calculated using a
  shared secret key to prevent spoofing.
- Quiet-Hours Policy: Suppresses non-critical alerts during scheduled hours (e.g., night shifts or weekends),
  while guaranteeing that HIGH-severity armed incidents immediately bypass suppression.

3.10 HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP OPERATOR REVIEW & TRIAGE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Immutable Audit Logging: Every operator action (Mark Reviewed, False Alarm, Escalate) is permanently
  recorded in the `review_actions` table with operator user ID, UTC timestamp, and justification notes.
- False Alarm Categorization: Operators marking an incident as a false alarm must select a root-cause
  category (Cleaning equipment, optical glare, benign carried object, playful interaction).
- Audit Export: Incident dossiers can be downloaded on demand as cryptographically verified JSON audit reports.

4. MATHEMATICAL FORMULATIONS & ALGORITHMIC SPECIFICATIONS
====================================================================================================

4.1 YOLO SPATIAL MULTI-TASK LOSS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The spatial object detector optimizes a composite objective balancing bounding box regression,
classification, and distribution focal loss:

    L_total = lambda_box * L_GIoU + lambda_cls * L_BCE + lambda_dfl * L_DFL

1. Generalized Intersection over Union (GIoU):
   Given predicted box B and ground truth box B_gt, let C denote the smallest enclosing convex bounding box:

       IoU = |B & B_gt| / |B | B_gt|
       L_GIoU = 1 - IoU + (|C \ (B | B_gt)| / |C|)

   Unlike standard IoU which yields zero gradient when boxes do not intersect, GIoU provides a continuous
   gradient driving non-overlapping boxes toward each other.

2. Binary Cross-Entropy Classification Loss (BCE):
   Evaluates multi-label weapon class assignment:

       L_BCE = - sum_c [ y_c * log(p_c) + (1 - y_c) * log(1 - p_c) ]

3. Distribution Focal Loss (DFL):
   Models box coordinates as continuous probability distributions over discrete bins rather than fixed
   delta coordinates, improving accuracy for partially occluded weapons.


4.2 BYTETRACK KALMAN STATE ESTIMATION & ASSOCIATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each active weapon candidate is modeled in continuous 2D camera coordinates by an 8-dimensional state vector:

    x = [u, v, s, r, u_dot, v_dot, s_dot, r_dot]^T

Where:
- (u, v): Center coordinates of bounding box
- s: Scale (area in pixels)
- r: Aspect ratio (width / height)
- u_dot, v_dot, s_dot, r_dot: Corresponding velocity derivatives

Kalman Prediction Phase:
    x_{t|t-1} = F * x_{t-1|t-1}
    P_{t|t-1} = F * P_{t-1|t-1} * F^T + Q

Association:
Detections and predicted track boxes are matched using the Hungarian Algorithm based on IoU distance:
    D(i, j) = 1 - IoU(B_det_i, B_track_j)


4.3 K-OF-N TEMPORAL CONFIRMATION ALGORITHM
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let W_t denote a fixed-size FIFO sliding window of boolean detection flags over the last N sampled frames:

    W_t = [d_{t-N+1}, d_{t-N+2}, ..., d_t],  where d_i in {0, 1}

The candidate object is promoted to an ACTIVE THREAT if and only if:

    Sum_{i=1}^N (d_i) >= K

Default operational parameters: N = 4 frames, K = 2 frames. Transient noise appearing for only a single
frame achieves Sum = 1 < 2 and is discarded before sounding the alarm.


4.4 S3D SEPARABLE 3D SPATIOTEMPORAL CONVOLUTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Standard 3D convolutions apply kernels of size (k_t x k_s x k_s), which requires heavy floating-point
multiplication (O(k_t * k_s^2 * C_in * C_out)).

S3D factorizes the 3D convolution into two decoupled operations:
1. Spatial Convolution: Applies a 2D spatial kernel (1 x k_s x k_s) extracting static spatial features.
2. Temporal Convolution: Applies a 1D temporal kernel (k_t x 1 x 1) capturing motion dynamics over time.

This factorization reduces computation by over 33% and improves generalization on human action datasets.


4.5 TEMPERATURE SCALING & PROBABILITY CALIBRATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Deep neural network softmax outputs frequently exhibit overconfidence. Given uncalibrated logit vector z:

    p_i = exp(z_i / T) / sum_j [ exp(z_j / T) ]

Where T is the learned temperature parameter (T = 1.28).
- When T > 1.0: Softens probability distributions, curbing false high-confidence predictions on edge cases.
- When T = 1.0: Standard softmax probability.


4.6 KINETIC MOTION DYNAMICS DIFFERENCING
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Given consecutive grayscale video frames I_{t-1} and I_t:

    Delta_t(x, y) = | I_t(x, y) - I_{t-1}(x, y) |
    E_kinetic(t) = (1 / (H * W)) * sum_{x,y} [ Delta_t(x, y) * Step(Delta_t(x, y) - theta_noise) ]

Where theta_noise suppresses camera sensor thermal noise. High kinetic energy spikes indicate violent struggles,
rapid limb swings, and running, which dynamically boost the calibrated violence action score.


4.7 EXPONENTIAL MOVING AVERAGE (EMA) SMOOTHING
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To prevent high-frequency frame jitter:

    S_smooth(t) = alpha * S_current(t) + (1 - alpha) * S_smooth(t-1)

Operational smoothing coefficient: alpha = 0.35.


4.8 DUAL-THRESHOLD HYSTERESIS STATE TRANSITIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To prevent alarm flickering when violence probabilities oscillate around the decision boundary:

    State(t) = ACTIVE   if Score(t) >= T_on (0.65)
    State(t) = ACTIVE   if State(t-1) == ACTIVE and Score(t) > T_off (0.40)
    State(t) = INACTIVE if Score(t) <= T_off (0.40)


4.9 ALERT STORM TOKEN-BUCKET RATE LIMITER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Protects operators from repeated alert spam during prolonged incidents:

    B_t = min(B_max, B_{t-1} + r * Delta_t)

Where:
- B_max = 6 tokens (maximum burst capacity)
- r = 0.1 tokens/sec (6 tokens replenished per minute)
- Each alert emission consumes 1 token. When B_t < 1, candidate alerts are merged into the active incident
  without triggering redundant sound alarms or webhook blasts.

5. DATABASE ARCHITECTURE & SCHEMA SPECIFICATION (SQLite WAL Mode)
====================================================================================================
Database file: `data/fasttrack.db` (Configured with PRAGMA journal_mode=WAL for high-concurrency read/write).

TABLE 1: `cameras`
- `id` (TEXT, PK): Unique camera identifier (e.g., 'cam-demo-01', 'cam-01')
- `name` (TEXT): Human-readable descriptive name (e.g., 'Main Gate - South Entrance')
- `source_type` (TEXT): Video adapter type ('file', 'webcam', 'rtsp')
- `source_ref_masked` (TEXT): Sanitized source URI or file path (passwords masked)
- `enabled` (INTEGER): Stream state (1 = active, 0 = disabled)
- `zones_json` (TEXT): JSON array of normalized polygon coordinates for include/ignore zones
- `thresholds_json` (TEXT): Camera-specific overrides for weapon and violence thresholds
- `schedule_json` (TEXT): Active operational hours and quiet-hours policy
- `created_utc` (TEXT): ISO 8601 creation timestamp

TABLE 2: `incidents`
- `id` (TEXT, PK): Unique incident identifier (e.g., 'inc-demo-101', 'inc-3a8f9c10')
- `camera_id` (TEXT, FK -> cameras.id): Source camera feed
- `severity` (TEXT): Triage severity ('INFO', 'LOW', 'MEDIUM', 'HIGH')
- `status` (TEXT): Review lifecycle ('new', 'reviewed', 'false_alarm', 'escalated')
- `start_utc` (TEXT): Incident start timestamp
- `end_utc` (TEXT): Incident conclusion timestamp
- `peak_weapon_score` (REAL): Maximum observed weapon confidence (0.0 to 1.0)
- `peak_violence_score` (REAL): Maximum observed violence action probability (0.0 to 1.0)
- `summary` (TEXT): Concise textual incident summary

TABLE 3: `events`
- `id` (TEXT, PK): Unique atomic event identifier (e.g., 'evt-001')
- `incident_id` (TEXT, FK -> incidents.id): Parent aggregated incident
- `camera_id` (TEXT, FK -> cameras.id): Camera feed
- `ts_utc` (TEXT): Precise event detection timestamp
- `event_type` (TEXT): Signal category ('weapon_detection', 'possible_violence', 'system_alert')
- `class_name` (TEXT): Detected object or action label (e.g., 'knife', 'gun', 'physical_altercation')
- `raw_score` (REAL): Raw uncalibrated model output
- `calibrated_score` (REAL): Temperature-scaled calibrated confidence
- `bbox_json` (TEXT): JSON array [x1, y1, x2, y2] bounding box coordinates
- `track_id` (INTEGER): ByteTrack persistent tracking ID
- `model_version_id` (TEXT): Neural network checkpoint identifier
- `evidence_id` (TEXT, FK -> evidence.id): Linked cryptographic evidence item

TABLE 4: `evidence`
- `id` (TEXT, PK): Unique evidence identifier (e.g., 'ev-001', 'ev-clip-101')
- `kind` (TEXT): Artifact type ('snapshot' or 'clip')
- `path` (TEXT): Relative file path on storage disk (e.g., 'evidence/snapshots/demo_knife_snapshot.jpg')
- `sha256` (TEXT): 64-character hexadecimal SHA-256 cryptographic digest of physical file
- `size_bytes` (INTEGER): Exact file size in bytes
- `encrypted` (INTEGER): Encryption state flag (0 = plaintext, 1 = AES-GCM encrypted)
- `created_utc` (TEXT): Creation timestamp
- `expires_utc` (TEXT): Data retention expiration timestamp

TABLE 5: `review_actions` (Append-Only Audit Trail)
- `id` (TEXT, PK): Unique action identifier
- `incident_id` (TEXT, FK -> incidents.id): Reviewed incident
- `user_id` (TEXT): Reviewing security operator username
- `action` (TEXT): Triage action ('mark_reviewed', 'mark_false_alarm', 'escalate', 'add_note')
- `note` (TEXT): Required operator justification / audit notes
- `ts_utc` (TEXT): Timestamp action was committed

TABLE 6: `users`
- `id` (TEXT, PK): Unique user identifier
- `username` (TEXT, UNIQUE): Login username
- `password_hash` (TEXT): Salted Argon2id cryptographic password hash
- `role` (TEXT): Access control role ('admin', 'reviewer', 'viewer')
- `active` (INTEGER): Account status flag (1 = active, 0 = disabled)
- `last_login_utc` (TEXT): Last authenticated login timestamp

9. EXAMINER VIVA & DEFENSE QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
====================================================================================================

Q1: Why did you decouple spatial weapon detection from spatiotemporal violence classification?
A1: A weapon is a static spatial object (a physical knife or firearm existing within a single frame),
whereas violence is a temporal action pattern (punches, struggles, and shoving unfolding across multiple
seconds). Conflating both into a single model leads to severe false alarms (e.g., a person holding an
umbrella flagged as violence, or a person hugging flagged as weapon possession). Decoupling them allows
YOLO to run at full camera FPS for spatial precision while S3D processes rolling 16-frame action clips.

Q2: How does the system prevent false alarms on cell phones, pens, or water bottles?
A2: We implement Benign Object Suppression and Full-Scene Multi-Class NMS. In standard setups where the
model is artificially restricted to class 43 (knife), the model cannot predict "cell phone", so faint
knife-like features on the phone's edge survive uninhibited. By running full-class competition, YOLO
identifies the object as a "cell phone" with high confidence (e.g. 0.88), naturally suppressing the weak
knife proposal. Additionally, spatial overlap suppression rejects weapon candidates that intersect with
benign everyday objects.

Q3: Why use SQLite instead of a heavier database like PostgreSQL or MongoDB?
A3: FastTrack AI is engineered for edge deployment directly on localized CCTV recording servers. SQLite
requires zero network socket overhead, zero external daemon processes, and operating in Write-Ahead
Logging (WAL) mode supports concurrent multi-threaded writes alongside continuous dashboard reads with
microsecond latency.

Q4: What happens if GPU video memory (VRAM) runs out during live surveillance?
A4: Both the weapon and violence detectors implement CUDA Out-of-Memory (OOM) recovery blocks. When an
OOM exception is caught, the system empties the PyTorch CUDA cache, transitions to CPU Host inference
mode, logs an operational warning, and continues processing without crashing the camera stream.

Q5: How is chain-of-custody guaranteed for forensic evidence in legal proceedings?
A5: Whenever an alert triggers, keyframes and video clips are written atomically to storage, and an
immutable SHA-256 cryptographic hash digest is computed and stored in the database. When evidence is
retrieved, the file is re-hashed on disk; if even a single byte has been modified, the hash verification
fails, displaying an immediate visual tamper warning on the forensic review screen.

Q6: Why are probabilities from weapon and violence models not summed together?
A6: P(weapon) and P(violence) are non-additive probabilities from disparate probability distributions.
Summing them (e.g. 0.40 weapon + 0.35 violence = 0.75) is mathematically meaningless and creates false
emergencies from two weak uncertainties. Instead, we use boolean state rules: a HIGH alert strictly
requires both signals to independently cross their calibrated operational thresholds.
